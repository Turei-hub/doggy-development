Four marks to choose from

Each one is drawn as clean vector, works in a single colour, and holds together at favicon size. Concept 02, Good Dog, was the one chosen — it is the mark used across the website.

01

The Squircle

A paw held in a rounded square. The safest of the four and the most flexible — it already looks like an app icon, drops straight into a social profile circle, and reads at any size. Least distinctive as a standalone mark.

02

Good Dog

My pick

A dog's face reduced to five shapes — two folded ears, a head, a chin, and a nose. This is the one people will remember and describe to a friend — it has a character rather than a symbol, which matters for a business built on one person your dog already knows. Warmest of the four, and the only one that isn't a paw print.

03

The Paw D

A solid D with the paw cut out of it — a monogram, not an illustration. The most grown-up of the four and the strongest as a stamp: on a van door, a poo-bag dispenser, or embroidered on a polo it holds up at a distance where the others go soft.

04

The Name Tag

A collar tag on its ring. The most literal to the business — every dog you walk is wearing one — and it gives you an obvious physical product: a real tag with your number on the back, clipped to every client's collar. The ring is the weak point at small sizes; below about 20px it starts to fill in.
